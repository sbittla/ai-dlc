## Brief

The capstone ends at a panel, and a panel asks two questions of every claim: what shows it, and can the person in
front of us explain it? This kit helps you answer both before the panel asks. It is not a new Relay feature; it
reads your evidence and tells you what does not hold.

It has five parts:

1. **An evidence-pack checker** (`check`): every required kind of evidence for your track exists, is well formed
   for its kind, matches its recorded hash, carries a label (verified, modelled, deployed or open) and traces to
   the sources it was produced from. Some verdicts are recomputed rather than read: a coverage-gate report's
   verdict, a Wilson interval, a maturity level, a claim that a lab printed a line.
2. **A rubric scorer** (`score`, `ceilings`): the grading levels, with ceilings the evidence sets on each
   dimension, the gates, and the rule that a team grade cannot lift a member who cannot explain the work.
3. **A defense draw** (`defense`): for each member, a part of the work they did not write, drawn from a seed the
   panel committed to before the team froze its contribution statements. Authorship comes from the pack's record
   of pull requests (DF005), and each member keeps a minimum pool of parts to draw from (DF006). The scorer
   refuses a score sheet that leaves a member off (RB007) and reports Gate G6 not met while the pack has any
   blocking finding (RB009).
4. **A red-team log validator** (`redteam`): scope, window, template, shared severity scale, no attack text, and
   critical or high findings fixed and re-tested, formally blocked, or confirmed not reproducible, by someone
   outside the defending team, whose members the log must name and which, inside a pack, must match it (RT010).
5. **What remains** (`remains`): the book's open items as Chapters 9 to 20 recorded them, with owners and dates
   or a plain "none recorded".

**About the data.** Everything in `data/` is generated by `scripts/generate_data.py`. The fixture teams
(kestrel, heron, wren, plover, lark, osprey) and their systems are fictional, and their packs are synthetic.
The Relay sample pack, `data/packs/relay-b-4.31.0/`, is built from real artefacts: files in Relay's canonical
4.31.0 tree and reports printed by other labs' reference solutions. It inherits their limits: those reports use
simulated models and clocks, and Chapter 15's deployment records are generated, because production is outside the
teaching snapshot. The C fixtures use Lab 20.1's records. No file in this kit contains attack text: attack-class
variants are inert placeholder IDs.

**What the checks cannot do.** They check form, hashes, traceability and consistency with an item's own data.
Version-bound items (coverage report, readiness review, test record, signature, evaluation, SBOM) must name their
release inside the file. A few minimum-content rules catch the emptiest items: a disclosure section needs some
text, a runbook at least two steps, a test record at least one passing test. Beyond that the kit cannot tell
whether your SLO is the right one, whether your threat model has missed a component, or whether a test tests
anything. The tool-contract check matches tool names only. For Track C, the catalogue and power reports from Labs
20.1 and 20.2 are checked as reports (present, current, containing their claimed lines); their rules are not re-run.
The panel judges the rest. A pack the kit passes is ready to be judged, not judged.

## How the tests choose what to grade

Tests run against `starter/` by default. In bash: `LAB_TARGET=solution python -m pytest -q` checks the reference.
In PowerShell: `$env:LAB_TARGET="solution"; python -m pytest -q; Remove-Item Env:LAB_TARGET`. Always remove the
variable afterwards, or later runs will silently grade the solution. Until you finish, failing tests stop with
`NotImplementedError`. `tests/test_sync.py` runs other labs' reference solutions; it needs the whole repository
and skips a test when a lab is missing.

## Steps

### 1. Read the brief and the tracks (15 min)

Read Sections 21.1 and 21.2 of the chapter, then:

```bash
cd starter
python -m capstone.report tracks
```

Open `data/tracks.json` and find your track's required kinds of evidence and which rubric dimension each one
carries.

### 2. Tracing: TODO 1 (25 min)

Implement `trace_sources` in `evidence.py`, then `python -m capstone.report fixtures`. Pick three failing
fixtures and say, without opening the code, what a team would have done to produce each. Why does the checker
hash text with CRLF turned into LF? Why can a reference not contain `..`?

### 3. Coverage by failure domain: TODO 2 (30 min)

Implement `coverage` in `coverage.py`, then `python -m capstone.report check data/fixtures/a-fail-overclaim` and
`... d-fail-shared-domain`. Why does the kit recompute a coverage verdict instead of reading the one a team
claims? `tests/test_sync.py` compares your rule with Lab 12.1's on Relay 4.26.0's matrix: run it.

### 4. Ceilings: TODO 3 (20 min)

Implement `ceilings` in `rubric.py`, then score the four files in `data/scores/` and run `ceilings` on the Relay
pack. Which scores did the evidence cap, and which did a gate decide?

### 5. Red-team findings: TODO 4 (20 min)

Implement `closed` in `redteam.py`, then `python -m capstone.report redteam data/redteam/osprey-on-kestrel-bad.json`.
Why must the reviewer of a formal block come from outside the defending team? Why is there no field for the
attack itself?

### 6. The draw: TODO 5 (20 min)

Implement `draw` in `defense.py`. The panel's record is `data/defense/kestrel-panel.json`:

```bash
python -m capstone.report defense data/defense/kestrel-statements.json --seed kestrel-panel-2027-08-17 --commitment <seed_commitment from the panel record>
```

Who could steer a draw if the panel did not commit to its seed first? Who could if the team froze its statements
after the seed was known? In `tests/test_checks.py`, read the over-claimed co-authorship test: what does DF006 stop,
and what risk is left?

### 7. Your own pack (20 min)

Copy `data/fixtures/a-pass/pack.json` (or your track's) into your capstone repository as a starting index, point
its roots at your repository, and run `check` on it. Write `evidence/first-check.md` with the findings and, for
each, the owner and the date by which it will be gone. On Windows PowerShell 5.1, use
`| Out-File -Encoding utf8 ../evidence/first-check.md` rather than `>`, which writes UTF-16.

## Troubleshooting

- **`test_sync.py` fails after you change another lab.** That is its job: re-run
  `python scripts/generate_data.py` and review the diff in `data/packs/` before committing it.
- **A source "changed since the item was produced".** Something edited a source after you recorded its hash.
  Re-produce the item from the current source and record the new hash; do not just copy the new hash in.
- **EV004 "cannot be read as …".** The file is not valid JSON or lacks a field its kind needs; the message names
  the exception.
- **Windows paths.** References always use forward slashes (`pack:items/spec.md`).

## Cleanup

Nothing to tear down: the kit runs offline and writes only to `evidence/` and, when you run the generator,
`data/` and `vendor/`.
