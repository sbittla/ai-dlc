## Brief

Four product teams at the company that builds Relay (34 engineers) have one quarter of delivery data with Chapter
1's definitions. The engineering director wants to know whether coding agents on the company's paved road would help
them, and has a draft plan: let teams volunteer, count lines of code and prompts, and look at a dashboard each week.

You will design the experiment properly and then read it out. First you estimate how much lead time varies, and
compare the power of four designs by simulation: teams against teams, engineers against engineers, and tasks
randomized within engineers in two teams or in all four. Then you check a pre-registration that fixes the outcome,
the randomization, the contamination risks, the power, the analysis and the guardrails before the start. Finally you
analyze the pilot exactly as registered.

**About the data.** Everything in `data/` is **generated** by `scripts/generate_data.py` from a fixed seed. The data
is synthetic, and the effects of agents in it were **configured** (`data/config.json`, repeated in
`data/README-data.md`). The readout recovers them with noise. It is a demonstration of method, not evidence about
any real coding agent. The variance components are estimated from the generated baseline; four assumptions the
baseline cannot inform are stated in `solution/experiment/power.py` and printed with every power table.

## What you will practise

- Choosing a **randomization unit** and naming its **contamination** risks.
- Computing **statistical power** by simulation for each design, from a baseline and stated assumptions.
- Writing a **pre-registration** that a check can hold you to: one primary outcome, no output metrics, a bound
  assignment list and no unplanned looks.
- Reading out a pilot **within engineer**, with a paired t-test and an exact sign test, and saying "no clear
  change" when the interval allows it.

## How the tests choose what to grade

Tests run against `starter/` by default. In bash: `LAB_TARGET=solution python -m pytest -q` checks the reference. In
PowerShell: `$env:LAB_TARGET="solution"; python -m pytest -q; Remove-Item Env:LAB_TARGET`. Always remove the variable
afterwards, or later runs will silently grade the solution. Until you finish, failing tests stop with
`NotImplementedError`. The power tests take about ten seconds.

## Steps

### 1. Read the brief and the data (15 min)

Read Section 20.5 of the chapter and Chapter 7's sections on paired comparisons and statistical power. Open
`data/prereg/prereg-draft.json` and list what you would change before reading on.

### 2. The baseline (15 min)

```bash
cd starter
python -m experiment.report baseline
```

Which variance component is largest? What does that mean for comparing teams with teams?

### 3. Power by design: TODO 1 (40 min)

Read `one_pilot_team_level` and `one_pilot_engineer_level` in `power.py`, then implement `task_level_differences`
and run `python -m experiment.report power`. Why is D1's power so low with four teams, and what single assumption
decides it? Why does D2 lose power as contamination grows? Why might you still prefer D3 to D2 or D4? Power belongs
to a test: compare D3's power with the paired t-test and with the sign test, and say which one a registration
must use.

### 4. Pre-register: TODO 2 (40 min)

Implement `check_prereg` in `prereg.py`, then `python -m experiment.report prereg --draft` and
`python -m experiment.report prereg`. REG004 and REG008 each combine all their problems into one finding. Read
`check_frozen` (REG011): why does a hash of the registration, held outside the pilot teams' reach, matter more than
the date in the file? For each of the draft's findings, say how it would have let the sponsor reach the conclusion
they wanted. Copy `templates/preregistration-review.md` into `evidence/` and complete it.

### 5. Read out the pilot: TODO 3 (35 min)

Implement `paired_t` in `analysis.py`, then `python -m experiment.report readout` and
`python -m experiment.report config`. Which outcomes show a clear change, and which do not? Where does the review
queue absorb the time the authors saved? The generator planted a change-fail harm: did the pilot see it, and what
does "no guardrail fired" prove? Why is the bootstrap interval narrower than the t interval with 15 engineers?

### 6. The memo (35 min)

```bash
python -m experiment.report readout > ../evidence/readout.md
```

On Windows PowerShell 5.1, use `| Out-File -Encoding utf8 ../evidence/readout.md` instead of `>`. Complete
`templates/readout-memo.md`. The memo states the primary result first, in the registered terms, and says what the
pilot could not detect.

## Sample of acceptable evidence (excerpt)

> With tasks randomized within engineer, change lead time showed no clear change: a ratio of 1.03 (0.91 to 1.17),
> paired t-test p = 0.619, with 6 engineers lower and 9 higher. Authors spent less time (0.69, 0.56 to 0.86), and
> reviewers more (1.29, 1.14 to 1.46); changes waited longer for review (1.42). The pilot was powered for a 20%
> change in lead time, so a smaller effect could hide in this result. No guardrail fired, but the configured +2.0
> point change-fail harm was invisible to a pilot of this size: its power against that harm was 0.10.

## Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Likely cause |
| --- | --- |
| D3's power is about 0.05 at every ratio | You did not apply the effect, or applied it to both arms; the difference carries effect × (1 − contamination). |
| Power differs from the golden values | Draw in the documented order: count, then each change's arm, then heterogeneity, then noise. |
| REG004 fires on the reviewed file | Compare the hash with the assignment list's SHA-256 only when one is given. |
| REG008 fires on "two looks with O'Brien-Fleming alpha spending" | Accept looks that name a spending function. |
| REG003 misses `commit_count` | Check secondaries against `ALLOWED_SECONDARY`, not only the banned list. |
| The interval is too narrow | Use the sample standard deviation (divisor k - 1) and the t quantile on k - 1 df. |
