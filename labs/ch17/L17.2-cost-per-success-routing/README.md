## Brief

On Wednesday 6 January 2027 Relay moved ticket triage to a reasoning model. Chapter 7's gate had passed it:
it got nearly half as many held-out tickets wrong. On Monday 8 February a finance review found that triage
had cost three times as much per ticket for a month. Chapter 17 tells the story (a composite). In this lab you
find out why the gate did not see it, compare three routing configurations on cost per correct triage, test
whether prompt caching helps, hold an abuse burst to a daily spend quota, and recalibrate Chapter 10's agent
budgets for the cache-write surcharge.

**About the data.** The tickets and the triage simulator are **Lab 7.1's**, copied unchanged, with one added
**configured** profile: `sim-reasoner`, whose accuracy was set to halve `sim-large`'s held-out errors on the
first call. How long it reasons, how fast each model writes, the abuse traffic and the incident's dates are
**configured** in `data/`; the reasoning lengths and output rate were chosen from four settings while the
held-out results for B and C were in view, so that the incident's cost ratio appears. The tables therefore
illustrate the method; they are not evidence that one configuration is better. Prices are Relay 4.28.0's dated assumptions (`relay.routing`, copied into `data/relay-4.28.0/`), and
the cache rules (512-token minimum, five-minute lifetime, writes at 1.25 times the input price, reads at 5% for the priced model; about 10% on most others)
are a provider's as documented in late 2026: check them before using any figure. Chapter 10's runs are
extracted from Lab 10.1 by `scripts/extract_ch10.py` and committed.

## What you will practise

- Replaying production rules (timeouts, retries, hand-over to a person) so that **cost per successful outcome**
  includes every billed call.
- Comparing configurations on quality with **paired statistics**, latency with percentiles, and cost per success.
- Checking a **token limit** against a timeout.
- Modelling **prompt caching** on real arrival times, including when it does not apply.
- Testing **spend quotas** against a **denial-of-wallet** burst, and seeing what a workspace limit costs real users.

## How the tests choose what to grade

Tests run against `starter/` by default. To check the reference, in bash: `LAB_TARGET=solution python -m pytest -q`.

## Steps

### 1. Read the incident and the routes (15 min)

Read Chapter 17's opening incident, ADR-042, and `data/relay-4.28.0/src/relay/routing/policy.py`. Which two
settings of a route decide whether a slow answer is paid for and thrown away?

### 2. Production rules: TODO 1 (35 min)

Implement `triage` in `production.py`, then `cd starter && python -m costlab.report gate`. This is what the
gate saw. Note B's cost per correct answer against A's.

### 3. Three configurations: TODO 2 (35 min)

Implement `summarize` and `paired` in `compare.py`, then `python -m costlab.report configs` and
`python -m costlab.report incident`. Why did B's quality gain shrink in production, and where did the extra
money go? Is C's gain clear of zero, and how many tickets carry it? Which route fails the route check, and why?

### 4. Caching: TODO 3 (25 min)

Implement `simulate` and `prefix_cost` in `caching.py`, then `python -m costlab.report caching`. Why does
caching save nothing for `triage-v3.1`? What would make it apply, and what does routing do to the hit rate?

### 5. Abuse and quotas: TODO 4 (30 min)

Implement `burst` in `abuse.py`, then `python -m costlab.report abuse > ../evidence/abuse.md` (PowerShell 5.1:
`| Out-File -Encoding utf8 ../evidence/abuse.md`). What does the rate limit allow one token to cost in an hour?
What happens to real users in the twenty-token scenario, and is that the right trade?

### 6. Agent budgets: TODO 5 (25 min)

Implement `costs` and `design_costs` in `agentcost.py`, then `python -m costlab.report agents`. Which budgets
change once the cache-write surcharge is priced, and why does the single agent lose most when caching is off?

### 7. Decide (15 min)

Fill in `templates/cost-decision.md` for one configuration: quality (paired), latency (p95), cost per correct
triage, the caching finding, the quota limits and what you would watch daily.

## Troubleshooting

- `NotImplementedError: TODO n` — expected until you implement TODO n.
- `ModuleNotFoundError: relay` — run from `starter/` or `solution/`; `costlab` adds `data/relay-4.28.0/src` itself.
- Results differ from the chapter — check `LAB_TARGET`, then rerun `python scripts/generate_data.py` and
  `git diff data/`; the data must not change.
- `UnicodeEncodeError` on Windows — set `PYTHONIOENCODING=utf-8`.

## Known limits

Every model is simulated, and the patterns in the tables were configured. Costs are expected values on 200
tickets; the paired results rest on few discordant tickets. Queueing is Lab 17.1's subject: here a ticket never
waits for a worker. The cache model ignores provider-side eviction, and quotas reserve a ceiling, not the mean.
